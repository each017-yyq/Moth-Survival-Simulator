How to play

1. Choose light or dark tree bark and set predator pressure to Low, Medium, or High.

2. Click Run next generation to let birds hunt the moths and allow survivors to reproduce.

3. Change the settings between generations to test a different environment. Use the population tracker to compare the percentages over time.

4. Click End simulation to see your results, or click Reset to start again. Random survival and reproduction outcomes mean each run can be different.

What happens in each generation?

1. Birds hunt moths. Moths that stand out against the bark have a higher chance of being eaten.

2. Moths reproduce sexually. A male can mate with multiple females, and each mated female produces two offspring, with a 20% chance of a third. Offspring inherit the color of one parent and are randomly assigned a sex.

3. The population tracker updates to show the new percentages.

What should you remember?

Individual moths did not change their wing color during the experiment. Instead, moths with a camouflage advantage were more likely to survive and reproduce. Because offspring inherited a color trait from a parent, the proportion of each color could change across generations.

This is a simplified model. It assumes one surviving male can mate with multiple females, and each mated female produces two offspring (sometimes three); real moths usually lay many eggs, and mating, fertility, and sex ratios are more complex. The survival chances are model settings, not real-world measurements. Random chance can affect the results, and real populations are influenced by many factors.
